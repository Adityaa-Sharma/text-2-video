STYLE: Black and white 35mm film, high contrast expressionist lighting, heavy film grain, Prague in 1910.

A thin young man in a dark suit and bowler hat, with large dark eyes and a pale narrow face, walks alone toward the camera across an old stone bridge lined with statues of saints. Thick morning fog swallows the far end of the bridge, and a huge castle looms faintly on the hill above him. Crows lift off from the statues as he passes. The camera slowly tracks backward in front of him. Sound of slow footsteps on stone, distant church bells, wind.

A small boy sits alone at the end of a long dark dining table, staring down at his plate. On the wall behind him the enormous shadow of a broad-shouldered man rises slowly until it covers the whole wall and the ceiling, swallowing the boy's own small shadow. The boy does not look up. A single hanging lamp sways slightly. The camera holds still, low, at the height of the table. Sound of a heavy chair scraping, a deep muffled voice, a ticking clock.

An endless office hall with hundreds of identical wooden desks in perfect rows, vanishing into darkness. Clerks in identical dark suits stamp papers in unison. Towers of paper files rise from the floor to the ceiling like pillars. In the middle sits the thin young man with large dark eyes, very small at his desk, looking up at the towers as loose sheets drift down around him like snow. The camera slowly rises above the rows. Sound of rubber stamps, typewriters clacking, paper rustling.

Night in a tiny bare room. The thin young man with large dark eyes sits hunched at a small desk by a window, writing fast with an ink pen by the light of one candle. Black ink spills from the pen and spreads across the page, creeping over the desk and up the walls like growing roots. He keeps writing without noticing. Dawn light slowly turns the window grey. The camera slowly pushes in toward his hand. Sound of a pen scratching on paper, a candle flame, a clock ticking.

A dim bedroom in early morning, thin light through lace curtains. On a narrow iron bed, under a rumpled white blanket, lies a huge dark beetle on its back, its thin legs waving slowly and helplessly in the air. A framed family photograph stands on the bedside table. The bedroom door is slightly open, and the shadows of three people stand motionless behind it, watching through the gap. The camera slowly drifts from the door to the bed. Sound of faint scratching, floorboards creaking, whispering behind the door.

Deep winter snow at dusk. The thin man in a dark coat and bowler hat, seen from behind, trudges up a snow-covered road toward a castle on a hill. With every step he takes, the road stretches and the castle glides farther away from him. Tall narrow doors stand alone in the snow on both sides of the road, each one closed. Snow falls heavily. The camera follows behind him at shoulder height. Sound of boots crunching in snow, howling wind, a distant door slamming.

A small stove burns in a dark empty room, its iron door open, a stack of handwritten manuscript pages waiting beside it. A hand reaches for the pages and stops. A window blows open, and the pages lift into the air, swirl out through the window and over the rooftops and spires of Prague at sunrise, and one by one the sheets of paper turn into white birds flying toward the light. The camera follows the pages out of the window and rises over the city. Sound of a crackling fire, fluttering paper becoming wingbeats, a single church bell.
